## Three case studies

### Flagship: [NYC 311: "Closed" is not "fixed"](https://github.com/Muhammad-Amjad-DA/nyc-311-closed-vs-resolved)
**Question:** when NYC says a complaint is closed, does the problem actually stay solved?

**Finding:** 46.5% of closed requests come back at the same address within 30 days. For heat and hot water it is **70%**, and 1,000 buildings produce **41%** of the repeats.

3.65M requests · DuckDB SQL · 7 tie-out tests, all passing · Power BI report spec with DAX · a 100-note hand-labelling sheet to test the closing-note rules

### [UK Retail: how much "returns" is really an order error?](https://github.com/Muhammad-Amjad-DA/uk-retail-leakage-retention)
**Question:** is the return rate as bad as the raw data says, and which customers should be kept?

**Finding:** the 2011 return rate looks like **4.84%**; after removing order-and-cancel errors the real figure is **2.31%**. The top 23.5% of customers (Champions) bring **69.5%** of revenue.

1.07M invoice lines · duplicates and credit-note errors documented · cohort retention and RFM · 6 tie-out tests

### [SaaS Churn: who costs the most, and who do we call first?](https://github.com/Muhammad-Amjad-DA/saas-revenue-churn-analysis)
**Question:** which churned customers cost the most revenue, and which active accounts are next?

**Finding:** Starter is 49% of churned customers but only **16%** of lost revenue. Business is **51%**. 31 active accounts ($28K MRR) need a call first.

SQL · Excel · Power BI · DAX. *Synthetic dataset, impact figures are illustrative and labelled as such.*

## How I work

1. **Business question first**: who is asking, and which decision changes?
2. **Clean and log**: every data issue and every rule is written down.
3. **Tie out**: key numbers are checked by SQL tests before I trust them.
4. **Recommend**: findings end in an action, a rough impact range and a Limitations section.

AI is a tool I use for drafting code and checking my wording. Each project has an AI log of what it got wrong and what I corrected.
