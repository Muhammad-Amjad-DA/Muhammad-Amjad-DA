## Three case studies

### Flagship: [NYC 311: When a Complaint Is "Closed", Does It Stay Solved?](https://github.com/Muhammad-Amjad-DA/nyc-311-closed-vs-resolved)
**Question:** when NYC says a complaint is closed, does the problem actually stay solved?

**Finding:** 46.5% of closed requests are followed by a new one at the same address and type within 30 days. For heat and hot water it is **70%**, and 1,000 buildings produce **41%** of the repeats.

3.65M requests · DuckDB SQL · 7 tie-out tests, all passing · 3-page Power BI specification with DAX · hand-validation workflow for the closing-note rules

### [UK Retail: Where Does Revenue Leak, and Who Is Worth Winning Back?](https://github.com/Muhammad-Amjad-DA/uk-retail-leakage-retention)
**Question:** is the return rate as bad as the raw data says, and which customers should be kept?

**Finding:** the 2011 return rate looks like **4.84%**; after removing order-and-cancel errors the real figure is **2.31%**. The 25.9% of customers who are Champions bring **70.8%** of revenue.

1.07M invoice lines · duplicates and credit-note errors documented · cohort retention and RFM · 6 tie-out tests

### [SaaS Churn: Which Customers Cost the Most, and Who Should We Call First?](https://github.com/Muhammad-Amjad-DA/saas-revenue-churn-analysis)
**Question:** which churned customers cost the most revenue, and which active accounts are next?

**Finding:** Starter is 49% of churned customers but only **16%** of lost revenue. Business is **51%**. 31 active accounts ($28K MRR) need a call first.

SQL · Excel · Power BI · DAX · a documented data-reconciliation log. *Synthetic dataset, impact figures are illustrative and labelled as such.*

## How I work

3. **Tie out**: key numbers are checked by SQL tests before I trust them.
4. **Recommend**: findings end in an action, a rough impact range and a Limitations section.

AI is a tool I use for drafting code and text. Each project has an AI log of what it got wrong and what I corrected.
